The Long Version

I started designing for the web in 2014, more or less by accident. I was studying CS, fell in with a small student magazine, and stayed up all night trying to make their HTML page feel like the printed cover.

It mostly didn't. But the part of me that cared about a typeface enough to argue with a stranger about it at 2 a.m. — that part hasn't really stopped working since. I finished the degree, talked my way into an apprenticeship with a small product studio called Field & Form, and spent the next seven years there learning to be useful to teams.

I left in 2022 to work for myself, around the same time our first kid was born. The plan, very loosely, was: take a small number of projects that interested me, ship them with care, write more often than was strictly comfortable, and be home for dinner. Three and a half years and one extra kid later, that's still the plan.

These days my work splits into roughly three parts: design for small and medium teams who are building something patient (most recently Acre Type and Folio); editing for Coda, the newsletter I co-write with Jordan Tate; and writing in this notebook, which is for me first and you second.

I'm interested in long timelines, careful interfaces, and the difference between a thing that's shipped and a thing that's good. I think about typography more than is reasonable. I take walks instead of meetings whenever I can get away with it.

Outside of work I read late, run early, and am bad at chess in a way I find mostly clarifying. I live with my partner Maya and our two kids — Esi (4) and Kofi (1) — who together have absolutely demolished any illusion I ever had about being a productive person before 7 a.m.
